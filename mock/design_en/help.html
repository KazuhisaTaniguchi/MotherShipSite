Getting started with MOTHERSHIP

This page walks you through installing on your Mac and connecting, then setting up voice input.
Work through it from the top and you'll be up and running.

On this page, “Mac” means the Mac you control remotely, and “device” means the iPhone, iPad, or Android device in your hand. There are two apps: MOTHERSHIP HOST, which goes on your Mac, and MOTHERSHIP, which goes on your device. (In the apps themselves, the names appear as “Mothership Host” and “Mothership.”) The figures are illustrations; the actual screens look different.

In a nutshell

  • Install MOTHERSHIP HOST on your Mac and turn on two permissions.
  • Install MOTHERSHIP on your device and scan the QR code on your Mac.
  • Tap Connect, and your Mac's screen appears in your hand. That's all it takes to use it at home.
  • To write by voice, install Aqua Voice and BlackHole 2ch on your Mac, then set Aqua Voice's microphone to BlackHole 2ch.
  • To use it away from home, install Tailscale on your Mac and your device.

How it works, and what to do

The app that turns your voice into text is Aqua Voice, a dictation app for Mac. Aqua Voice listens to your Mac's microphone and transcribes what it hears. So the voice arriving from your device has to be handed to Aqua Voice as if it were microphone audio, and that takes a virtual microphone. That's BlackHole 2ch.

There are six things to do:

  1. Install Aqua Voice

  2. Open Terminal

  3. Install Homebrew (the tool used to install BlackHole 2ch)

  4. Install BlackHole 2ch

  5. Set Aqua Voice's microphone to BlackHole 2ch

  6. Try talking from your device

These are third-party products. Aqua Voice, Homebrew, and BlackHole are made by companies and people other than MOTHERSHIP (they're not included with MOTHERSHIP). How you install them, and what their screens say, may change.

3-1Install Aqua Voice

Download the Mac version from the official Aqua Voice site (aquavoice.com) and install it. It supports both Apple silicon and Intel-based Macs.

Once it's installed, first make sure it works normally at your Mac. Click somewhere you can type, such as a note. Hold the fn key on your keyboard, speak, then let go. If what you said is typed in, you're all set.

3-2Open Terminal

From here on, you'll use Terminal, an app that comes with every Mac. Terminal lets you run your Mac with typed commands. It may look intimidating, but all you do is paste in the commands on this page exactly as they are.

  1. Hold the ⌘ (Command) key and press the space bar. The Spotlight search field appears.

  2. Type “Terminal” and press return.

  3. A text-only window opens. Paste a command here and press return to run it.

3-3Install Homebrew

Homebrew is a free tool for installing software on a Mac. You'll use it to install BlackHole 2ch.

  1. Check whether it's already installed. Run this command:

    brew --version
    • If you see “Homebrew” followed by a version number, it's already installed. Skip ahead to 3-4 Install BlackHole 2ch.
    • If you see “command not found,” it isn't installed yet. Go on to the next step.

  2. Install Homebrew. Run this command (it's a single line):

    /bin/bash -c "$(curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Homebrew/install/HEAD/install.sh)"
    • When you see “Password:”, type the password you use to log in to your Mac and press return. Nothing appears on screen as you type, but your input is being entered.
    • When you see “Press RETURN/ENTER to continue,” press return.
    • This takes a few minutes. Along the way, a component called Command Line Tools is installed as well.
    • When you see “Installation successful!”, it's done.

  3. Run the “Next steps” commands. Near the end, you may see “Next steps:” followed by two or three lines of commands. If you do, copy those lines and run them one at a time. They set things up so you can use Homebrew from Terminal.

    For example, they look something like this (this is only an example; use the ones shown on your own screen):

    echo >> ~/.zprofile
echo 'eval "$(/opt/homebrew/bin/brew shellenv)"' >> ~/.zprofile
eval "$(/opt/homebrew/bin/brew shellenv)"

  4. Make sure it's installed. Run this command once more. If you see “Homebrew” and a version number, it worked.

    brew --version

3-4Install BlackHole 2ch

  1. In Terminal, run this command:

    brew install --cask blackhole-2ch
    • If you're asked for a password, type your Mac's password and press return.

  2. Restart your Mac. BlackHole 2ch won't work until you restart (the installer also tells you a restart is required when it finishes).

  3. Make sure it's installed. Open System Settings > Sound > Input. If BlackHole 2ch is in the list, it's installed.

It still works as usual at your Mac

You might wonder: “If Aqua Voice's microphone is set to BlackHole 2ch, won't it stop hearing me when I talk at my Mac?” Don't worry.

As long as MOTHERSHIP HOST is running, you can use Aqua Voice at your Mac just as before. When you use Aqua Voice at your Mac, MOTHERSHIP HOST passes the audio from your Mac's microphone to BlackHole 2ch. Hold fn and speak, and your words become text as always. There's no need to switch microphones.

  • Your Mac's speaker and microphone settings don't change. Installing BlackHole 2ch simply adds one more route for audio.
  • The microphone used at your Mac is the one selected in System Settings > Sound > Input. Switch to an earbud mic or anything else, and it just works.
  • Your Mac's microphone is used only while Aqua Voice is listening.
  • While you're speaking from your device, only the audio from your device is passed to Aqua Voice.

Three things to keep in mind
  • Microphone permission: The first time, MOTHERSHIP HOST asks for Microphone permission. Choose Allow.
  • When MOTHERSHIP HOST isn't running: What you say at your Mac won't reach Aqua Voice. To be safe, turn on Open at Login in MOTHERSHIP HOST (5 Keep your Mac awake). On days you don't use MOTHERSHIP HOST, set Aqua Voice's microphone back to your original mic and everything works as it did before.
  • Sound input: If the input in System Settings is set to BlackHole 2ch, your voice at the Mac isn't passed along. Keep the input set to your usual microphone.

3-6Try talking from your device

  1. Connect to your Mac from your device.

  2. On your Mac's screen, tap where you want the text to go (a note, an AI's input field, and so on).

  3. Hold the 🎤 button on screen while you speak, then let go when you're done. The first time, your device asks for microphone permission; allow it.

  4. After a moment, what you said is typed on your Mac.

4Use it away from home (Tailscale)

To connect from outside your home, you'll use Tailscale. Tailscale is a service that securely connects devices in different places, and it's free for personal use. You don't need to change any router settings.

  1. Create a Tailscale account.

  2. Install the Tailscale app on your Mac and your device, and sign in to the same account on both.

  3. On your home Wi-Fi, connect to your Mac with MOTHERSHIP once. Your device remembers how to reach your Mac from outside.

  4. Try it before you leave. Turn off Wi-Fi on your phone so it's on cellular, then tap Connect in MOTHERSHIP. If your Mac's screen appears, you're all set.

Your device, awayCellular or outside Wi-Fi
TailscaleEncrypted tunnel
Your Mac at homeMOTHERSHIP HOST

Input (device → Mac)Screen (Mac → device)

No MOTHERSHIP servers in between. No router setup, either.

When you're out, just make sure Tailscale is turned on on your device and tap Connect. Even if your Mac shows “○ Not found on home network (connects via Tailscale when away),” you can go ahead and tap Connect.

If it won't connect Under “Enter address manually” on your device, try entering your Mac's Tailscale name (for example, my-mac.tail1234.ts.net). You can find the name in the Tailscale app.
Your Mac's screen while you're connected from outside

When you connect from outside, the screen of your Mac at home is hidden automatically (so people at home can't see it). When you disconnect, your Mac locks.

You can change this with the setting on your device, “Automatically hide the home Mac's screen when connecting from outside,” and the one in MOTHERSHIP HOST, “Lock the Mac on disconnect if its screen was hidden.”

6Troubleshooting

At home, your device can't find your Mac
  • Make sure your Mac and your device are on the same Wi-Fi network.
  • Make sure the MOTHERSHIP HOST icon is in your Mac's menu bar. If it isn't, open MOTHERSHIP HOST.
  • On iPhone or iPad, go to Settings > Privacy & Security > Local Network and make sure MOTHERSHIP is turned on.
